## Goal

One Hydrograf instance serves whole large basins, one or several: the Barycz (5,543.2 km²) and the Bóbr (5,881.8 km², about 44 km² of it in Czechia) together, later the Odra. The data are continuous: one address, one database, delineation and parameters across each whole basin. A basin is composed of many runs, its parts, each within a fixed cell budget under the 8 GB cap of a bootstrap: the method composes many analyses into one whole instead of fitting an area into one run. The method does not depend on the cell size as long as every JCWP's window fits the budget: a basin has one grid, the budget counts cells, and a finer grid needs more parts — down to one JCWP per part, no further (D1). The cell size is the author's choice per basin, never finer than its coarsest DEM input (D3's floor; the author's answer 1 of 2026-11-24): 5 m for the Barycz and the Bóbr, since at 1 m 80 of their 123 JCWP exceed 90 M grid cells on their own (the largest, 683.2 km², 1,492 M); a basin runs at 1 m only when every JCWP's window fits the budget at 1 m, and a basin wanted finer than its instance's grid gets its own instance (Grid and cell size 4, 5). The analysis is a single-flow-direction D8 tree. The priority is the work done with adequate quality, not the fewest runs.

### Hydrograf at `939b55f`

- **One run per instance.** The unique index `idx_processing_runs_one_active` allows one active run (`migrations/versions/005_processing_runs.py:70-71`); `publish_run` (`core/unit_run_store.py:516`) deletes every DEM-derived `stream_network` row and every `stream_catchments` row (`:600`, `:603`).
- **ADR-071 and its amendment** (`docs/decisions/0071-pgw-units-and-boundary-event-routing.md`): PGW units own the outputs (tiles, records, resume), not the computation. Fills per unit window never agreed on the seams at 5 m (closed basins wider than any halo, flats broken differently by each window; the halo grew from 500 m to 16 km, `:65`), so the fill and D8 run once on the whole grid at about 18 B per cell, and „a larger area needs the fill itself to change, not windows” (`:71`). Burning, smoothing, buildings and lakes run on the whole DEM too: a window changed 37 cells by up to 5.2 m where a stream enters it (`:52`).
- **The run's grid in one array.** `fill_whole_grid` (`core/unit_hydrology.py:182`) reads the run's whole DEM (`:211`, through `core/raster_io.py:199`, `src.read(1)`), the box of the set's sheet frames (`utils/kartograf_set.py:298`; the mosaic at `scripts/bootstrap.py:579-583`); the fill and the routing scratch are removed after the run, and the run keeps per-owner tiles of `02_filled`, `03_flowdir`, `04_flowacc`, `06_streams` and the other rasters (`scripts/process_dem_units.py:14-24`, `:794-803`), and a report with the peak memory of each stage (`:17-18`).
- **Units.** A PGW polygon owns the cells whose centre it holds; the layers are `zlewnie_jcwp_rzecznych` and `pozostale_obszary_jcwp` (`core/unit_ownership.py:34-35`), from a `--units` file beside the set (`scripts/bootstrap.py:1696-1705`). Owners that overlap by more than 1 m² are refused (`_require_disjoint`, `core/unit_ownership.py:354`, `:414-426`; `_OVERLAP_TOLERANCE_M2`, `:44`), and so is a valid cell inside the area, deeper than one cell from its boundary, that no polygon covers (`:376-397`); the other valid cells without a polygon, beyond the area or in that band, go to the nearest owner (`nearest_valid_owners`, `:97`, called at `:401`).
- **Rasters.** Per-owner tiles and one VRT per raster on the run's grid, every valid cell in exactly one tile, verified before anything is replaced (`core/unit_rasters.py:24-26`, `_write_vrt` at `:386`, `write_unit_mosaics` at `:550`); the API reads the VRTs of the run that `active_run.json` names (`core/active_run.py:121-140`, `core/config.py:240-252`).
- **Graph.** In memory, keyed by `(threshold_m2, segment_idx)` (`core/catchment_graph.py:156-157`, `:282`) and loaded from the whole `stream_catchments` (`:225-233`); its docstring sizes it at ~87k nodes (`:114`), Barycz 1 had 163,750 and the 7 parts 806,916 (`REPORT.md:96`). Since 0.5.1 it loads in a thread of its own when the API starts and the requests answer 503 until it is loaded (E5b-01 fixed); it is still the whole `stream_catchments` of one run.
- **Limits.** Stream thresholds of 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10 km² (`core/constants.py:26`); precise-mode windows up to 40 M cells (`core/raster_service.py:42`), network windows up to 60 M (`core/network_delineation.py:115`), hydrographs up to 250 km² (SCS-CN, `core/constants.py:19`).
- **Burning.** `dem.stream_burning: main` (default `all`, `core/config.py:353`) burns the OT_SWRS_L main courses (`PRZEBIEG = 'ciek główny'`, never a side arm) that are rivers or carry `IDENTYFIKATORMPHP`, every OT_SWKN_L that carries it, whatever it connects, and no OT_SWRM_L (`core/hydrology.py:378-386`). The ends of the burned lines are joined into nodes within one cell (`_line_nodes`, `:672`), and each line is oriented by the rules of ADR-077 (`_line_directions`, `:1014`): rule 1 orients an MPHP watercourse towards the node where it meets its receiver, a line whose identifier is a proper prefix of its own (`_mouth_directions`, `:802`); rule 2 orients the lines of one MPHP identifier, else of one name, down the DEM (`_watercourse_directions`, `:954`, the keys at `:985-988`).
- **PGW units carry no receiver.** Hydrograf reads PGW's catchment polygons by `MS_KOD` (`_read_pgw_polygons`, `core/unit_ownership.py:152`) and no other PGW layer. Nothing in the GeoPackage says which JCWP a JCWP drains into: in the scenario's file (`/data/nmt/e2e-2026-11-15/geo/raw/pgw/`) the catchments carry `MS_KOD`, `AREA` and `UWAGI`, and the 548 river lines of `jednolite_czesci_wod_powierzchniowych_rzecznych` carry `MS_KOD`, `Nazwa_JCWP` and `DLUG`.
- **Line topology.** Besides `_line_nodes`, the pipeline orders lines by Kahn's algorithm over their nodes (`_flow_order`, `core/hydrology.py:1072`), and ADR-077's rule 1 reads only the nodes, the MPHP identifiers and the lengths, no DEM (`_mouth_directions`, `:802`). The diagnostic `scripts/check_stream_directions.py`, outside the pipeline, reads the line layers that reach into an area (`read_lines`, `:160`), splits a line where another line's end lies on its middle (`split_at_t_junctions`, `:239`), joins ends within 0.5 m (`join_ends`, `:300`), names a node where two or more lines begin a bifurcation, `rozwidlenie` (`node_kinds`, `:341-385`, `:379`), marks a line from a bifurcation to a dead end (`:1099`) and joins the lines of each watercourse end to end (`trace_watercourses`, `:683`).
- **Accumulation.** `flw.upstream_area(unit="cell")` (`core/hydrology.py:2243`); pyflwdir's `accuflux(data)` accumulates from initial values.
- **Resolution.** A set's NMT comes at `1m` or `5m` (`utils/kartograf_set.py:72`), from `nmt/pl_1992_<resolution>_evrf2007` only (`:77`); one `--resolution` per run (`scripts/bootstrap.py:1806-1811`). ADR-087 (`docs/decisions/0087-a-sheet-missing-at-the-run-resolution-from-a-finer-nmt-of-the-set.md:18-20`) builds a missing 5 m sheet from 1 m data with `align_to_cellsize` (`utils/raster_utils.py:195`, called at `scripts/bootstrap.py:575-577`): `gdalwarp -r average` at the run's cell size over the sheet's frame grown outward to whole cells of the lattice, nodata −9999 left out of the mean (`utils/raster_utils.py:263-287`). The lattice is the corner offset that the run's sheets of its cell size share, (0, 0) when the run has none (`:234-244`), and a sheet coarser than the run is refused (`:252-256`). The 1 m cells nest in the 5 m lattice: M-33-19-B-c-2-2 has its 1 m `xllcenter` at 246,673.00 and its 5 m copy its `xllcorner` at 246,672.5, so a 5 m cell is the mean of the valid 1 m cells within it, 25 when all are valid. Thresholds are areas in m², turned into cells by the run's cell size (`core/unit_hydrology.py:599`, `core/cell_parameters.py:132`), and the readers take the cell size from the raster (`core/cell_parameters.py:125-127`, `core/raster_service.py:128`).
- **Network method** (ADR-080, `docs/decisions/0080-network-delineation-at-control-points.md:136-143`, `:164-166`): control points, reaches, the przyrzecze as an element that enters the junction below it unrouted, and `hydrolog-network/1` to Hydrolog.
- Decision D3 of [the Hydroni plan](2026-11-06-hydroni-instance.md) processed units in windows with an upstream-first hand-off; ADR-071's amendment replaced that with the whole-grid fill. This plan returns to fills per window, for parts much larger than units, and accepts a measured seam instead of an exact one.

## Design

### Side channels (D15)

1. Detection is a step of the network topology (below), once per basin, on its split, joined and directed lines, before any part runs. A line is a side channel when:
   - BDOT10k marks it a side arm, `PRZEBIEG = 'ramię boczne'`, which `main` already leaves out (`core/hydrology.py:380-384`);
   - it begins at a bifurcation, a node where two or more burned lines begin (the diagnostic's `rozwidlenie`, `scripts/check_stream_directions.py:379`), and does not continue the river: the line that continues it belongs to the watercourse of the line that ends there, by its MPHP identifier, else its name, as ADR-077's rule 2 groups lines (`core/hydrology.py:985-988`);
   - or its watercourse reaches burned rivers at both ends, a loop off a river, for which ADR-077's rule 1 finds two mouths and turns the halves towards both (`_mouth_directions`, `core/hydrology.py:802`).
2. Every line that continues a side channel downstream, to where it ends or rejoins a river, is cut with it. A bifurcation that neither rule decides keeps its lines and gets a warning.
3. A side channel is not burned, is no inlet and makes no flow path; the seam report lists one that crosses a part boundary, as it lists ditches. The cells that would drain through it follow the DEM: the D8 of the filled DEM gives each cell one direction, wherever the terrain leads, through a channel cut into the DEM too. No water is routed or split at the GIS stage.
4. The topology's GeoPackage lists each side channel with the rule that cut it.

### Network topology (D16)

Once per basin, before the JCWP are grouped and before any download or bootstrap. It reads vectors only, the basin's PGW GeoPackage and the BDOT10k hydro layers of its counties, and no NMT sheet.

1. The network: the burned BDOT10k lines (`main`, `core/hydrology.py:378-386`) that reach into the basin, split at T-junctions and joined at their ends into nodes.
2. Direction without the DEM: ADR-077's rule 1 orients every MPHP watercourse towards its receiver; any other line keeps its digitized direction, BDOT10k's downstream (ADR-077's last rule). The JCWP's main rivers are MPHP watercourses [INFERENCE: BDOT10k carries their identifiers], so rule 1 orients them the same way when the preparation along lines later applies every rule, with the DEM, to burn them.
3. The side channels are cut (D15).
4. Outlets: a JCWP's main river is the burned watercourse whose `IDENTYFIKATORMPHP` its code names — `MS_KOD` is `RW`, the region, the type and the MPHP code of the JCWP's stretch, and the river's identifier is that code without its trailing odd digits [INFERENCE: the MPHP convention — odd digits for a river's stretches, even for its tributaries — verified on the two basins' 118 river JCWP, not nationwide] (`RW60001116599` → `16`, the Bóbr; `RW60000616389` → `1638`, the Bobrzyca; `RW600006163794` → `163794`, the Młynówka; 117 of the 118 river JCWP of the two basins found among the scenario's BDOT10k identifiers) — and its outlet is that watercourse's last exit from the JCWP's polygon along the flow. PGW's river line (`jednolite_czesci_wod_powierzchniowych_rzecznych`, by `MS_KOD`) is the check: where it follows another burned watercourse, or the code names none (Kamienica, `RW60000316329` → `1632`), the JCWP is listed and the line it follows decides. A reservoir of `pozostale_obszary_jcwp`, without a river line, has its lake's outflow as its outlet (D4). A JCWP whose river line follows only a side channel that D15 cuts has no burned main river: its cells follow the DEM, it can be no outlet edge, and it goes into the part of the JCWP whose river the side channel leaves [proposed]. The Młynówka is no such case: a tributary (Facts).
5. Receivers: the JCWP whose polygon the network enters at the outlet; none for the JCWP whose outlet leaves the basin. Every other JCWP has exactly one receiver, and the graph has no cycle; a JCWP with no receiver or two stops the basin as a failed partition check does, listed with what was found.
6. Output: a GeoPackage per basin with the directed lines, the side channels, every JCWP's outlet point and the graph's edges; the grouping, the inlet table and the admin panel read it.
7. In Hydrograf (H2): a module `core/basin_topology.py` and a command `python -m scripts.partition_basin` [proposed names]. They take over the diagnostic's line topology (`read_lines`, `split_at_t_junctions`, `join_ends`, `node_kinds`, `trace_watercourses`; `scripts/check_stream_directions.py:160`, `:239`, `:300`, `:341`, `:683`), which the diagnostic then imports, and reuse the pipeline's `_select_burned_features` (`core/hydrology.py:367`), `_mouth_directions` (`:802`), `_flow_order` (`:1072`, for the topological order of the parts) and `_read_pgw_polygons` (`core/unit_ownership.py:152`).

### Parts (D1, D2, D16)

1. A basin's closed set brings its area, the NMT sheets, the BDOT10k counties and the HSG raster; the PGW GeoPackage comes from `--units` beside the set, as today. Its JCWP (`zlewnie_jcwp_rzecznych`, `pozostale_obszary_jcwp`) are the units of ADR-071 and the nodes of the JCWP graph.
2. The grouping follows the graph, after the topology. A part is any set of whole JCWP whose window stays within the budget; it may hold several separate subtrees, each leaving the part by its own outlet edge, into one part or several. Hydrograf's grouping grows parts from the basin's outlet up the graph and makes only outlet edges that pass the check: a JCWP whose outlet edge would carry less than 10 km² (`core/constants.py:26`) goes into its receiver's part, together with the JCWP above it (e.g. Stary Bóbr, 0.34 km², into the part of the Bóbr's JCWP it enters). The grouping avoids cuts at reservoirs, lakes and confluences (D4) and keeps pond complexes in one part. A partition given as a file, as the scenario's by MPHP code group, is valid when it passes the check. A part is named by the `MS_KOD` of the JCWP of its largest outlet edge; the partition (part → JCWP, the topological order) is a file of the basin, the same for one set and budget [proposed].
3. A part's window is its outline buffered by 1 km, as a rectangle on the basin's grid; cells beyond the buffered outline are nodata. At 90 M grid cells and 42–61 % data per window, the Barycz and the Bóbr's Polish part need about 5–6 parts each at 5 m [INFERENCE]; at 1 m a part's window is at most 90 km².
4. Preparation: what works per cell (buildings, repaired holes) runs per window; what works along lines (the burning depth, ADR-072's smoothing, ADR-077's directions by every rule, lake outflows) runs once per basin on the topology's lines, side channels cut, with the DEM sampled along them, and is rasterized into every window, so both windows of a seam burn the same values [proposed: the answer to ADR-071's `:52`].
5. Fill and D8 per window, with the code of today's whole-grid fill (`fill_whole_grid`) and the window's edge as outlets.
6. Ownership: an outlet of a part is a cell of one of its outlet edges or the basin's mouth, never the window's edge. A cell belongs to a part when its D8 path in the part's window reaches an outlet of the part without passing an inlet of the part. The first run of a seam claims so within its JCWP outline and the 1 km band beyond it; when the second part of the seam publishes, the band is reconciled in that publish's transaction: a cell both claim, or none (windows that disagree on a flat, a path that leaves the window), goes to the part whose JCWP holds its centre; the earlier part's band tiles and the VRT are rewritten, the moved cells are listed in the seam report, and no accumulation is recomputed (the seam is measured, not exact, D1). Every cell then has one owner, as the tiles need [proposed].

### Partition check (D16)

It runs on every partition, the one the grouping grows and one given as a file (T1 gives the scenario's), after the topology and before any download or bootstrap of the basin. An outlet edge is a JCWP of a part that drains into a JCWP of another part; the part graph has an edge per outlet edge. Every failure is an error:

1. Coverage: every JCWP of the basin lies in exactly one part.
2. Main rivers: every outlet edge lies on a burned main-river line where it crosses the JCWP boundary, at the outlet of the JCWP's main river (Network topology 4; for a reservoir, on the river leaving its lake), never on a ditch, a canal without an MPHP identifier or a side channel.
3. Order: the part graph has no cycle, so the parts have a topological order (`_flow_order`).
4. Threshold: every outlet edge carries at least 10 km², the largest stream threshold (`core/constants.py:26`), as the area of the JCWP above it in the graph, its own included. The grouping makes no smaller one (Parts 2). A partition given as a file is never changed: one with a smaller edge fails, and the message names the receiver's part to move the JCWP into [proposed].
5. Area: the parts' JCWP overlap by at most 1 m² and leave no land of the basin's area uncovered deeper than one cell from its boundary, the rules that the bootstrap's ownership already enforces as errors (`core/unit_ownership.py:354`, `:376-397`), now checked on the vectors before any download. They stay errors because an overlap or a gap gives cells two owners or none, which the tiles cannot hold; the PGW polygons of both basins have neither (Facts). JCWP land outside the area is only a warning, with its area and place: an area may leave out the Czech land until the follow-up (D11), and E7-GAP-01's DIBAVOD cut, 188.1 km² of Polish land, would have shown here.
6. Budget: every part's window (Parts 3) holds at most the budget of D2, 90 M grid cells on the basin's grid. The grouping makes no larger part; a partition given as a file fails with the part's name and its window in cells. A reference run (Acceptance 1's whole Orla) may pass the budget with `--budget-override` [proposed name] when it stays under the cap; its report records the override.
7. A failure writes no partition, and a part's bootstrap refuses a basin without a passed partition. The message lists every failing JCWP with its receiving JCWP (codes, names, outlet points, the area above), or the place of the gap or overlap; it is not a seam-report warning (D10 covers the seams only).

A part may send outlet edges into several parts, and a receiver may take inlets from several parts, and several from one, some into one cell. This is allowed (D16): the part graph stays acyclic, each edge has its own inlet record, and the attribute pass follows the topological order, so nothing more is needed.

The scenario's grouping of the Bóbr passes checks 1 to 5: its six outlet edges (Facts) lie on burned main rivers, carry 16.80 km² (the Młynówka) or more, and form 1 → 2b → 3 ← 2a; part 2b takes four inlets from part 1, two of them into one cell, and part 3 two into one cell, from 2a and 2b. Its windows of 108.9–196.7 M grid cells fail check 6, so it is the check's test case, not a run (Acceptance 2). E7-GAP-03's defect was the mosaic, not the grouping: part 1's window is now its outline buffered by 1 km as a rectangle (Parts 3), and the Bóbr between the mouths is 2b's, in 2b's own window. The grouping fails as soon as one JCWP lies in two parts, or with a cycle: the upper Bobrzyca (`RW600003163859`) moved into part 2b drains into 16389 in part 1, which drains into 16599 in part 2b (1 → 2b → 1).

### Grid and cell size (D2, D3)

1. A basin has one grid. Its cell size is never finer than the coarsest among its DEM inputs, the NMT sheets of its set that cover it (D3's floor: 5 m when any of them is 5 m, 1 m only when all are 1 m); at or above that floor it is the author's choice per basin, given by `--resolution` (`scripts/bootstrap.py:1806-1811`) at the basin's first part (5 below; the author's answer 1 of 2026-11-24). Its lattice is that of those sheets, whose corners agree modulo the cell size. The basin registry records both at the basin's first part; every part, every re-run and the inlet table use them [proposed].
2. A finer input is averaged onto the grid by ADR-087's method, `align_to_cellsize` (Facts): `gdalwarp -r average` at the grid's cell size over the input's frame grown outward to whole cells of the lattice, nodata left out of the mean; a 5 m cell is the mean of the valid 1 m cells within it. An input coarser than the grid cannot occur, since the grid is never finer than the coarsest input, and `align_to_cellsize` refuses one anyway (`utils/raster_utils.py:252-256`).
3. One change for the parts: `align_to_cellsize` takes the lattice from the run's own sheets, (0, 0) when the run has none of its cell size (`utils/raster_utils.py:234-244`), so a part whose window holds only 1 m sheets in a 5 m basin would land on another lattice. Every part gets the basin's lattice instead.
4. The basins of one instance share its grid, fixed in the registry when the first basin is registered, so one VRT per raster serves them all: a later basin with a coarser input is refused, a finer one is averaged onto the grid; a basin wanted at a finer cell size gets its own instance.
5. Resolution-agnostic within the budget: the method works at any cell size, fixed per basin, at which every JCWP's buffered window stays within 90 M grid cells — about 2,250 km² of window at 5 m and 90 km² at 1 m, where only the 43 smallest of the two basins' 123 JCWP fit alone. So the Barycz and the Bóbr run at 5 m, their 1 m sheets averaged onto it (2 above); a basin runs at 1 m only when every JCWP's window fits the budget at 1 m, else the registry refuses it, naming each JCWP over the budget and its window in cells; a basin wanted finer than its instance's grid gets its own instance (4 above; the author's answer 1 of 2026-11-24). Thresholds stay areas in m² (`core/unit_hydrology.py:599`), and the readers take the cell size from the raster (`core/cell_parameters.py:125-127`).

### Memory budget (D2)

1. The budget is 90 M grid cells per window under the 8 GB cap, counted with nodata: the fill reads the whole window into one array (`core/unit_hydrology.py:211`, `core/raster_io.py:199`), and the fill, the D8 and the later stages allocate on the window.
2. Its margin: Barycz 1's window of 188.6 M grid cells (111.0 M with data) completed under the 8 GB cap; 90 M is under half of it and below every window of the scenario. That peak, like every part's, sat at about 98 % of the cap, so it bounds the need without measuring it [INFERENCE of the report].
3. Every run's report keeps its peak memory per stage (`scripts/process_dem_units.py:17-18`); a part that exceeds the cap becomes smaller parts (D2).

### Inlets (D4, D5)

The inlet table comes from the vectors alone, once per basin and set and before any part runs, so both runs of a seam read the same cell of the basin's grid. Each outlet edge gives its receiver one inlet: the inlet's cell is the upstream river's last cell at the outlet point, and the receiver's run starts the river at the next cell down. A receiver has as many inlets as outlet edges enter it, from one part or several. Two inlets may lead into one cell, as the upper Bóbr and the Żeliszowski Potok at 258368, 375472; their records combine there as at a confluence (Order and attribute pass 3).

| Case | Inlet |
|---|---|
| The main river | the burned BDOT10k watercourse with the MPHP identifier of the outlet edge's JCWP, oriented by ADR-077 and the MPHP order; the cell of the basin's grid that holds its intersection with the part boundary |
| Several intersections (meanders across the boundary, a line along it) | the last exit along the flow |
| A reservoir or lake on the cut | the lake's outflow, where the outflowing line leaves the OT_PTWP_A polygon |
| A tributary of the receiver joining less than 200–500 m from an inlet | the grouping moves the cut (the JCWP on both sides into one part); where the budget forbids it, the inlet stays, flagged. Two inlets that meet, both from parts above, need neither (the upper Bóbr and the Żeliszowski Potok) |
| An unburned ditch or canal (OT_SWRM_L, OT_SWKN_L without an MPHP identifier) or a side channel crossing the boundary | none; the seam report lists it |
| The CZ border, at this stage (D11) | where the burned line reaches the border on the PL DEM; its upstream area unknown, its delineations marked `catchment_truncated` |

No other watercourse is an inlet, and a secondary inlet still cannot exist (D5), however many inlets a receiver has:

- Every inlet is an outlet edge: a JCWP's main river at its outlet (check 2) with at least 10 km² above it (check 4), so its river exceeds every stream threshold.
- A watercourse without a JCWP of its own lies inside the JCWP of the stretch it joins, since PGW's JCWP are the catchments of their stretches, and so inside the part where it joins: it is never an inlet.
- Check 4 counts JCWP polygons; the upstream run's record gives the D8 area, and a record under 10 km² is a warning in the seam report.
- What remains is a burned watercourse that crosses a part boundary where the JCWP divide and the DEM disagree. Within the 1 km buffer the D8 ownership gives its cells to the part where it joins. Where its catchment reaches beyond the downstream window, the seam report lists it with the area beyond the window that drains through it: a warning only, with no inlet, no record and no re-run.

Each upstream run checks its inlets: its owned cell of the highest accumulation near each crossing lies within D4's N cells of the inlet, else a warning in the seam report.

### Order and attribute pass (D5)

1. A part's geometry needs no other part. Each run accumulates with pyflwdir's `accuflux` from initial values: one cell for every cell the part owns, zero for every other cell of the window, and at each inlet the seed — the record's upstream cells where the record exists, else the cells of the JCWP above the inlet in the graph — so no cell of the band is counted twice and the parts' areas add to the basin's. Its fill, D8, streams at every threshold, segments and sub-catchments are then final whatever the order of the runs.
2. Only the attributes along the river below each inlet need the record of the part above: the upstream area, the longest flow path and hydraulic length, the distance to the source, the Strahler order, and the part's own outlet records for the parts below. Only they keep the topological order of the part graph (Partition check 3): the runs go upstream first where they can, as T1's chain does, so each run applies the records of every part above it.
3. The attribute pass carries a later change down, part by part in the topological order: after an upstream part is re-run, or after a part ran before a part above it. Along the D8 path from each inlet to the part's outlets it adds the change of the inlet's upstream area, takes the longer of the local and the inlet's longest flow path and distance to the source, and recomputes the Strahler order with the inlet's; where the paths of several inlets meet, their areas add, the longest path wins and their Strahler orders combine as at any confluence. It updates those rows (`upstream_area_km2`, `strahler_order`, the flow distances), the cells of those paths in the raster tiles and the stream tiles, and writes the part's outlet records for every part below it. It touches one path per inlet (tens of kilometres, about 10⁴ cells at 5 m), not a raster: no fill, no D8, no stream extraction.
4. A part run before a part above it publishes the PGW estimate below each such inlet with a warning in its record and the seam report; the attribute pass clears it once the part above is published.

### One database (D7, D8)

- A basin registry: the basin's id and name, its project and set (their identity as `instance.json` records it today), its area, grid (cell size and lattice), partition and parts.
- `basin_id` and `part_id` on `processing_runs` (the one-active index per part), on the DEM-derived `stream_network` rows, `stream_catchments`, `land_cover`, `soil_hsg`, `bdot_streams`, `depressions`, the sewer tables and every other table a bootstrap fills for its area (H3 makes the inventory; precipitation stays upserted by point).
